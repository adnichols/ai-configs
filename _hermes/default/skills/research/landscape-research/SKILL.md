# Landscape Research

Use this when Aaron asks questions like “does anything like this exist?”, “who is doing X?”, “map the ecosystem around Y”, “is there a market/category here?”, or asks for research on organizations/products/programs that solve a broad problem.

## Workflow

1. **Restate the hypothesized category in operational terms.** Break the ask into the functional components that would count as a match, e.g. request intake, matching, funding/credits, delivery, ongoing support, governance, etc.
2. **Search for both exact and adjacent categories.** Exact matches are often rare; also look for adjacent models such as accelerators, pro bono marketplaces, grants/credits, volunteer corps, fellowships, managed services, and open-source communities.
3. **Use primary sources first.** Prefer official organization pages, program pages, public project directories, FAQs, and press releases. Use secondary sources to discover names or fill gaps, but do not treat them as authoritative when a primary page is accessible.
4. **Classify findings by fit, not just by keyword match.** For each candidate, identify:
   - what it actually provides;
   - which part of the user's hypothesized category it covers;
   - where it falls short;
   - whether it is a closest match, adjacent model, infrastructure/credits provider, or not really a match.
5. **Look for live proof of mechanism.** Public project listings, application pages, program benefits, cohort pages, recipient lists, and partner directories are stronger evidence than broad mission statements.
6. **Synthesize the gap.** Answer whether the exact thing exists, whether pieces exist, who is closest, and what differentiated opportunity remains.
7. **Keep the final brief structured and decision-oriented.** Start with the answer, then closest matches, adjacent models, gaps, and practical next steps or opportunity shape.

## Output pattern

- **Short answer / thesis**: one paragraph.
- **Closest matches**: 1–3 orgs/products with evidence and fit assessment.
- **Adjacent models**: grouped by type.
- **What does not seem to exist**: explicit category gap.
- **Opportunity / next steps**: what to study, partner with, or build.
- Include source URLs inline or in bullets.

## Pitfalls

- Do not equate “AI credits/discounts” with “AI-enabled service fulfillment.” Distinguish infrastructure access from actual project delivery.
- Do not overclaim nonexistence from search results. Say “I did not find…” and explain the evidence boundary.
- Avoid a flat list of organizations; Aaron usually needs the ecosystem shape and the gap.
- If search engines block or rate-limit, pivot to known primary domains, official program pages, public directories, and link extraction from loaded pages rather than stopping.
