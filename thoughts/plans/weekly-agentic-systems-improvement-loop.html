Weekly Agentic Systems Improvement Loop

Execution plan for adding a bounded, evidence-driven second-order loop to Aaron's weekly review: capture outcomes from agentic coding and operating workflows, identify one repeated controllable failure, test one narrow process change against benchmark fixtures, and present it in Doct for human approval. The system proposes; it does not silently rewrite, install, merge, or activate its own operating rules.

Product-owner context

Key conclusion: add a second loop around the existing coding and operating workflows, not autonomy inside them. The minimum useful product is one evidence-backed, reversible process experiment per week. It must preserve Aaron's approval at the points where a proposal becomes authoritative configuration, a merged change, an installed workflow, or a new scheduled behavior.

Aaron already has substantial first-loop machinery: Hermes supervises work, Pi executes repo-based development, Doct provides durable plans and comments, C-Core is the intended signal surface, and ai-configs is the authoritative source for managed Hermes configuration. What is missing is a consistent way to turn actual outcomes from those workflows into small, testable changes to the workflows themselves.

The article that prompted this plan describes an outbound system that logs outcomes, lets an agent propose one scoring or prompt change, evaluates the change, and opens a pull request for human review. The transferable pattern is the separation between the work-producing loop and the loop that edits its rules. For Aaron's system, the proposal surface should initially be Doct plus a concrete candidate diff, because many improvements are judgment-based and the ai-configs repository intentionally works directly on main rather than using routine feature branches.

Decision and target loop

First loop versus second loop

LoopResponsibilityAuthority
First loopResearch, plan, delegate, code, review, validate, signal, and resolve operational work.Existing Hermes/Pi/Doct/C-Core workflows and their current gates.
Second loopInspect first-loop outcomes, diagnose repeated friction, propose one narrow process change, evaluate it, and ask for a decision.Read-only over evidence; write access only to proposal artifacts and candidate patches. No live config install, merge, external send, or production mutation.

Proof levels

  • proven_by_eval: deterministic fixture replay shows the candidate fixes the target case without guardrail regression.
  • supported_by_evidence: repeated outcomes support the diagnosis and fixture replay is directionally positive, but a monitored trial is still required.
  • review_required: the proposal depends materially on taste, communication quality, model behavior, or sparse evidence and must not claim proof.

Evidence and current-system fit

  • ai-configs/AGENTS.md establishes that this repository works directly on main by default and that active reviewed plans are semantic HTML files under thoughts/plans/.
  • The repository declares _hermes/default as the source copy for managed Hermes config and requires export/verify/install discipline through .claude/scripts/hermes_config_sync.py.
  • The managed Hermes bundle includes skills, scripts, and cron definitions, while runtime state, sessions, logs, databases, checkpoints, and generated output are intentionally excluded.
  • Existing Good Morning automation already demonstrates a useful pattern: deterministic collection, Doct plan publication, and a durable comment listener. The weekly tuner should reuse that shape rather than invent a parallel review surface.
  • The existing dev:reflect command updates a single plan after a phase, but it is phase-local and plan-centric. This plan adds cross-run evidence accumulation and weekly process-level proposals.
  • C-Core is the intended signal system, but known noninteractive auth constraints mean the MVP must not depend on C-Core reads being available. C-Core integration is additive after the local evidence contract works.

Evidence limitation: current agent/session logs were not treated as a clean historical dataset for this plan. The MVP therefore starts prospective and manual-first rather than pretending a reliable baseline can be reconstructed from inconsistent past telemetry.

Scope and non-goals

In scope

  • A compact operational law defining allowed evidence, edits, proof claims, and authority boundaries.
  • A versioned outcome schema and append/validate tooling.
  • A small benchmark fixture set covering normal, failure, stale-evidence, and guardrail cases.
  • Deterministic weekly aggregation and candidate-selection logic.
  • An agent synthesis step that proposes exactly one conceptual change and cites evidence.
  • A Doct HTML proposal artifact with stable anchors, a candidate diff, proof classification, risks, and Aaron's decision surface.
  • A manual four-week pilot, followed by optional weekly scheduling.
  • Optional C-Core links/exports after the local contract is proven.

Non-goals

  • No agent self-merge, self-install, self-deployment, or automatic editing of live Hermes/Pi behavior.
  • No optimization for raw task count, agent turns, token volume, or speed as a standalone success measure.
  • No automatic mining of every chat, repository, or private note in the MVP.
  • No replacement of Todoist, Obsidian weekly review, Doct plan review, or C-Core signal ownership.
  • No all-at-once modification of planning, model routing, prompting, validation, and signal handling.
  • No scheduling until at least two manual tune-ups have been read end-to-end and the four-week pilot exit criteria are met.

Data, evaluation, and proposal contracts

Outcome record

  "recorded_at": "2026-07-20T15:00:00Z",
  "workflow": "pi-coding-workflow",
  "work_class": "bugfix",
  "subject": "repo-or-system-safe-identifier",
  "result": "completed_with_rework",
  "phase_reached": "validation",
  "human_interventions": 2,
  "rework_cycles": 1,
  "verification": {
    "required": true,
    "passed": true,
    "evidence_refs": ["doct://...", "session://...", "github://..."]
  },
  "failure_class": "unverified_external_dependency",
  "reason": "Plan assumed noninteractive auth was available.",
  "tags": ["planning", "auth", "context"],

    "recorded_by": "human|workflow|agent",

Required rules: no empty reason, no future timestamp, no unrecognized enum, no secret-like values, and no success claim without evidence when verification is required. Records are immutable after append; corrections append a superseding record that references the original ID.

Candidate selection

  1. Validate all records in the review window.
  2. Group by workflow, phase, failure class, and controllable process surface.
  3. Compare the current week with a trailing four-week window when enough data exists.
  4. Reject candidates that are one-off, outside operator control, missing evidence, already under active remediation, or primarily a project/product defect.
  5. Require two supporting outcomes by default; allow one severe reliability/safety incident with an explicit severity override.
  6. Rank candidates by repeated human attention cost, correctness/reliability impact, reversibility, and evaluation quality.
  7. Select at most one proposal. An empty week is a valid result.

Scorecard and guardrails

Target measuresGuardrails
Human interventions, rework cycles, plan revisions after execution starts, stalled/lost-state runs, time from approved plan to verified result, signal-to-resolution time.Acceptance criteria verified, review escapes, evidence completeness, destructive/permission boundary violations, duplicate execution, stale or unowned signals, customer/security risk.

No single blended score decides whether a change is good. A target improvement is rejected if a relevant guardrail regresses or the evidence is too sparse.

Weekly proposal

Each proposal must contain: observed problem, cited evidence rows, confidence and sample caveat, one hypothesis, one conceptual change, exact source files that would change, candidate diff, baseline eval, candidate eval, guardrail result, proof classification, risks, rollback, monitored trial length, and a human decision of approve/revise/reject/gather-more-evidence.

Weekly review experience

  1. The deterministic runner validates the current week's records and produces a data-quality report.
  2. It computes recurring failure clusters and a short ranked candidate list.
  3. The synthesis agent receives only the validated packet, the operational law, current fixtures, and allowlisted candidate source files.
  4. The agent chooses zero or one candidate, proposes one change, and creates a candidate patch without applying it.
  5. The evaluator replays baseline and candidate behavior against fixtures and guardrails.
  6. A Doct artifact is created or updated with stable comment anchors and a canonical URL.
  7. A short weekly-review pointer says either “no evidence-backed change this week” or links the one proposal.
  8. Aaron approves, revises, rejects, or asks for another week of evidence.
  9. If approved, implementation occurs through the normal reviewed-plan/config workflow—not inside the tuner run.

Empty-week behavior: the system must prefer no proposal over a weak proposal. It publishes a compact evidence-quality summary and does not manufacture a process change to satisfy the cadence.

Acceptance criteria

  1. A versioned append-only outcome log records agentic coding and operating-workflow results with reasons and provenance at the time outcomes are known.
  2. Malformed, future-dated, secret-bearing, provenance-free, or unsupported success records fail closed without changing the log.
  3. The weekly runner selects zero or one repeated controllable failure and explains why other candidates were rejected.
  4. Every proposal changes one conceptual process surface and cites the exact outcome records that support it.
  5. Baseline and candidate evaluations are machine-readable, fixture failures are inspectable, and relevant guardrail regressions reject the candidate.
  6. Subjective or sparse-evidence changes are labeled supported-by-evidence or review-required rather than machine-proven.
  7. The generated candidate patch is isolated and cannot apply itself, edit live Hermes state, merge, install, deploy, or schedule another job.
  8. The Doct proposal has a canonical production URL, stable comment anchors, verified metadata, active lifecycle, drained startup queue, and durable listener ownership.
  9. A repeated weekly run is idempotent and does not duplicate outcomes, run directories, or Doct proposal documents.
  10. The loop remains useful when C-Core, Doct publication, session evidence, or another optional source is temporarily unavailable; it reports the missing source honestly and preserves retryable state.
  11. At least four manual weekly cycles are reviewed before scheduling, including at least two end-to-end read-throughs of every diff and evidence row.
  12. An accepted change is observed in the following week and may be reverted if its target outcome fails to improve or a guardrail regresses.
